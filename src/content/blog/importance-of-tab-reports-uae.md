Testing, Adjusting, and Balancing (TAB) is one of the most overlooked yet critical steps in HVAC commissioning. In the UAE's extreme climate, an unbalanced system does not just waste energy, it creates uncomfortable hot spots, accelerates equipment wear, and can fail authority inspections.

Here is why TAB matters and what you should expect from a professional TAB report.

## What Is TAB?

TAB is the process of measuring and adjusting airflow and water flow rates through an HVAC system to match the design engineer's specifications. It involves:

- Measuring air velocities at diffusers and grilles
- Balancing ductwork using dampers
- Adjusting chilled water flow rates at coils
- Verifying pump and fan performance curves
- Documenting all readings against design values

Without proper TAB, even a perfectly designed system will underperform.

## Energy Efficiency in the UAE Climate

Abu Dhabi's summer temperatures regularly exceed 45 degrees Celsius. An unbalanced HVAC system works harder than necessary, driving up electricity consumption. Common symptoms include:

- Some rooms too cold while others remain warm
- Chillers running at maximum capacity constantly
- High humidity in certain zones
- Excessive noise from fans and dampers

Professional TAB typically improves system efficiency by 15 to 25 percent, paying for itself within the first year through reduced DEWA bills.

## Regulatory and Consultant Requirements

Major Abu Dhabi developers and engineering consultants require TAB reports as a condition for Practical Completion. Our reports are prepared in accordance with:

- CIBSE Code A: Air Distribution Systems
- ASHRAE Guideline 111
- SMACNA TAB Standards

These standards ensure your documentation will be accepted by authorities including DMT, DOT, and private consultants like Parsons, AECOM, and MZ Architects.

## When Should TAB Be Performed?

TAB should be conducted:

1. After new HVAC installation, before handover
2. Following major renovations or system extensions
3. When occupancy changes significantly
4. If energy bills increase without explanation
5. Every 3 to 5 years as part of preventive maintenance
